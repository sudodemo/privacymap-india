# PrivacyMap India — User FAQ & Terminology

## Purpose

This guide explains PrivacyMap India in plain language for users who are not privacy, legal or technology specialists.

PrivacyMap is an assessment and readiness tool. It helps an organisation understand how it handles personal data, identify areas that may need attention, plan improvements, record governance decisions and collect evidence of remediation.

PrivacyMap is not a certification service and completing an assessment does not by itself establish legal compliance.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Getting started

#### What is PrivacyMap India?

PrivacyMap India is a browser-based privacy assessment tool designed to help organisations understand their personal-data processing and prepare for the Digital Personal Data Protection framework in India.

#### Who should use it?

Business owners, managers, privacy teams, HR, IT, security teams, compliance teams, process owners and other people who understand how the organisation handles personal data can contribute. You do not need to be a privacy lawyer or software developer.

#### What information should I enter?

Answer based on how your organisation actually works. Use the best available evidence rather than guessing. If something is unknown, record it as unknown where the assessment allows it and follow the resulting recommendation.

#### What is expected from me?

Provide honest, organisation-specific answers; identify the people responsible for decisions and actions; provide realistic dates; review identified findings; decide how risks will be handled; obtain required approvals; and provide evidence when actions are completed. The aim is to understand and improve the real situation, not to obtain the highest possible score.

#### What should I do if I do not know an answer?

Do not guess simply to improve the result. Use an available unknown/not-known option where provided, or confirm the information with the relevant process, IT, HR, security, legal or business owner.

### Scores and results

#### What is the DPDP readiness score?

The score is an assessment indicator generated from the answers and mapped controls. It is intended to show relative readiness and areas needing attention. It is not a government score, legal certification, audit opinion or guarantee of compliance.

#### What score should we try to achieve?

There is no legally prescribed PrivacyMap score that proves compliance. The practical goal is to address identified gaps, reduce material privacy risk, establish appropriate governance and evidence, and move the assessment toward a consistently strong readiness position. A high score with incomplete or inaccurate answers should not be treated as proof of compliance.

#### Why can a finding be high risk?

Risk reflects the combination of the potential impact of a privacy issue and the likelihood or exposure indicated by the assessment answers. A high risk does not automatically mean the organisation has violated the law; it means the issue deserves stronger attention or review.

#### What are Key Privacy Findings?

Key Privacy Findings are the principal issues identified by the assessment. Other report sections may refer back to the same findings. Clicking a finding title should take the user back to its original entry in Key Privacy Findings.

### Assessment workflow

#### What are the assessment steps?

The workflow uses the following stages. Later stages become available when the information required for their prerequisites is complete.

1. **Organisation & industry** — identify the organisation, assessment context and industry.
2. **Business type** — select the type of organisation or business operation being assessed.
3. **Business process** — identify the relevant process, such as registration, payments, recruitment or customer support.
4. **Data entry / collection channels** — identify where personal data enters the organisation, including web, email, messaging apps, APIs and AI assistants.
5. **Personal data categories** — identify the types of personal data involved.
6. **How the data is handled** — describe relevant handling, access, storage, sharing, retention and other processing practices.
7. **Privacy risk assessment** — assess the conditions that may create privacy findings and risks.
8. **Risk treatment & action** — decide what should be done about identified risks and assign actions.
9. **Residual Risk Decision & Approval** — record the residual-risk decision, rationale, owner and review information. Formal approval, where required, is a separate governance activity.
10. **DPDP Mapping** — review how findings and controls relate to the applicable DPDP readiness areas.
11. **Governance / approval** — record accountable governance decisions and required approvals.
12. **Remediation** — track planned or outstanding actions toward completion.
13. **Evidence & Closure** — provide or reference evidence, verify remediation and close items when appropriate.

#### Why does a later step sometimes remain locked?

PrivacyMap uses prerequisites to prevent incomplete information from being carried into later stages. A locked step does not necessarily mean there is a privacy violation; it means the information required by the workflow has not yet been completed.

### Owners and responsibilities

#### What is an Assessment Owner?

The person coordinating the overall assessment. They help make sure the assessment is completed, questions reach the right people and the resulting actions are followed up.

#### What is a Process Owner?

The person responsible for the business process being assessed. They should understand how the process actually collects, uses, stores or shares personal data.

#### What is a Risk Owner?

The person accountable for managing an identified privacy risk and ensuring that an appropriate response is decided and followed through.

#### What is a Treatment Owner?

The person responsible for implementing a specific risk treatment or corrective action.

#### What is an Evidence Owner?

The person responsible for providing, maintaining or locating evidence that supports completion of a treatment or control.

#### What is an Approver or Management Approver?

A person with the authority to formally approve a risk decision or other governance decision. Approval should only be recorded by someone authorised by the organisation.

#### What is a Reviewer or Verifier?

A person who checks whether a treatment, control or evidence actually meets the expected requirement. A reviewer does not automatically become the action owner.

#### Can one person have several roles?

Yes. Smaller organisations may have one person performing several roles. The important point is that the responsibility is clear and, where separation is required by internal governance, the roles should be assigned to appropriate different people.

### Dates

#### What is the Assessment Date?

The date associated with the assessment itself. It establishes when the assessment snapshot was prepared or performed.

#### What is a Target or Due Date?

The date by which an action, treatment or decision is expected to be completed. It is a commitment or target, not proof that the work has already been completed.

#### What is a Review Date?

The date when a decision, risk, control or treatment should be reviewed again. A review date does not necessarily mean the item is due for closure on that date.

#### What is a Treatment Due Date?

The expected completion date for a specific risk treatment or action.

#### What is an Evidence Verification Date?

The date on which someone checks the submitted evidence and confirms whether it supports the claimed completion or control status.

#### What is a Closure Date?

The date on which an item is formally considered closed after the organisation's required completion and verification conditions have been met.

### Statuses and decisions

#### What does Not Started mean?

The activity has not yet begun.

#### What does In Progress mean?

Work has started but the required outcome has not yet been completed.

#### What does Complete mean?

The required information or activity for that workflow stage has been completed according to the application's criteria. Complete does not automatically mean approved or verified.

#### What does Pending mean?

Something still requires completion, review or approval. For example, a residual-risk decision can be recorded while formal management approval remains pending.

#### What does Approved mean?

An authorised approver has formally accepted the relevant decision according to the organisation's governance process.

#### What does Rejected mean?

An authorised decision-maker has not approved the relevant proposal or decision. The organisation should follow its governance process to determine the next action.

#### What does Requires Review / Attention mean?

The information indicates that the user should inspect the item, provide additional information or take a recommended action.

#### What does Verified mean?

A reviewer has checked the relevant evidence or completion claim and found it satisfactory against the applicable verification criteria.

#### What does Closed mean?

The item has completed the organisation's required treatment, verification and closure process. Closure should not be used simply because an action was started.

### Risk and treatment

#### What is a Finding?

A privacy issue or condition identified by the assessment that requires attention, review or treatment.

#### What is a Risk?

The potential for a privacy issue to cause harm, exposure or an unwanted outcome, considered using the application's risk model.

#### What is Inherent Risk?

The assessed risk before considering the effect of controls or treatments.

#### What is Residual Risk?

The risk that remains after existing or planned controls and treatments are considered. Residual risk does not mean that all risk has been eliminated.

#### What is Likelihood?

An indication of how likely the relevant risk scenario is considered to occur under the assessment model.

#### What is Impact?

An indication of the potential consequence or seriousness if the relevant risk scenario occurs.

#### What is a Risk Score or Risk Rating?

An assessment indicator used to help prioritise privacy issues. It should be interpreted with the underlying finding, context and evidence rather than treated as a standalone legal conclusion.

#### What is Risk Acceptance?

A documented decision to retain a residual risk under defined conditions and with appropriate accountability. Acceptance is a governance decision, not proof that the risk is harmless or that legal obligations do not apply.

#### What is a Risk Treatment?

An action or set of actions intended to reduce, avoid, transfer or otherwise manage a risk.

#### What is a Treatment Plan?

A plan describing what the organisation intends to do about a finding or risk, including actions, ownership, status and expected completion.

### Governance, evidence and closure

#### Why do I need an owner and review date?

Privacy work needs accountability and follow-up. An owner identifies who is responsible for the action or decision, while a review date establishes when it should be reconsidered.

#### Why is management approval sometimes required?

Some residual-risk decisions may need an accountable authority to review and approve them. Completing a risk decision and obtaining formal approval are separate governance activities. PrivacyMap does not treat a pending approval as an automatic approval.

#### What is Governance?

Governance is the roles, decisions, accountability, approvals and oversight used to manage privacy risks and obligations.

#### What is Evidence?

Evidence is information that supports a claim that a control, action or remediation activity exists or has been completed.

Examples include policy references, procedures, screenshots, tickets, training records, configuration records, contracts, approvals or other appropriate organisational records.

#### Is completing an action the same as verifying it?

No. An action can be marked complete by the responsible person, but evidence may still need to be reviewed. Verification confirms that the claimed completion or control is supported by appropriate evidence.

#### Is verification the same as closure?

No. Verification checks evidence or completion. Closure is the final governance decision that the item's required treatment, verification and closure conditions have been satisfied.

### Industry, processes and data entry

#### What is Industry and Business Type used for?

Industry and business type provide context so PrivacyMap can present relevant business processes and improve the usefulness of its intelligence. They do not automatically determine that an organisation is compliant or non-compliant.

#### What are Data Entry / Collection Channels?

They describe where personal data enters or is collected by the organisation, such as web forms, mobile apps, email, telephone, messaging apps, social media, physical forms, APIs and AI assistants.

#### Why is AI prompts / AI assistants a collection channel?

People can enter personal data into AI tools. PrivacyMap therefore treats AI prompts / AI assistants as a first-class data-entry channel so organisations can consider what information is entered, why it is entered, who provides the service, retention/training settings where known, and other relevant processing facts.

Using an AI assistant does not automatically create a privacy finding.

#### Why are WhatsApp and Telegram grouped together?

Messaging products change over time. PrivacyMap therefore uses a general Messaging Apps category and can optionally record the specific provider/platform. This avoids creating a separate assessment field for every messaging product.

### Continuity and data integrity

#### Can I resume later?

Yes. Assessment continuity supports local saving and resume. Export/import and restore capabilities are also available where provided by the application.

#### Can someone edit an exported JSON file?

A JSON file is data, not a tamper-proof signature. Someone with access to the file can potentially edit it. PrivacyMap validates imported structure and values to reduce unsafe or malformed input, but import validation cannot prove that the contents are truthful or originally produced by an authorised person. Organisations should use appropriate internal controls for assessment packages.

### Privacy, security and legal boundaries

#### What is Evidence & Closure?

Evidence & Closure records proof that an action or control was addressed and verified. It helps distinguish an intended treatment from a treatment that can actually be supported by organisational evidence.

#### Does PrivacyMap send my assessment answers to PrivacyMap servers?

The assessment is designed to process assessment responses locally in the browser. Users should still review the application's Privacy Assurance information and their own browser/device environment.

#### Does PrivacyMap provide legal advice?

No. PrivacyMap provides assessment guidance and readiness information. Where an important legal interpretation or organisation-specific decision is required, obtain appropriate professional advice.

## Terminology

### Privacy and legal terms

- **Personal data:** Information relating to an identifiable individual.
- **Data Principal:** The individual to whom personal data relates.
- **Data Fiduciary:** An organisation or person that determines the purpose and means of processing personal data.
- **Data Processor:** A party that processes personal data on behalf of another organisation.
- **Processing:** Activities performed on personal data, such as collecting, storing, using, sharing or deleting it.
- **Consent:** A permission mechanism used where processing relies on consent and the applicable requirements are met.
- **Notice:** Information provided to a person about relevant personal-data processing.
- **Data minimisation:** Limiting collection and use to what is relevant and necessary for the intended purpose.
- **Retention:** How long personal data is kept.
- **Deletion:** Removing personal data when it is no longer required, subject to applicable requirements and legitimate retention needs.
- **Cross-border transfer:** Transfer or access involving personal data moving to or being accessed from another jurisdiction.
- **DPDP:** The Digital Personal Data Protection framework in India.

### Risk and governance terms

- **Finding:** An identified privacy issue or condition requiring attention, review or treatment.
- **Risk:** The potential for a privacy issue to cause harm, exposure or an unwanted outcome.
- **Inherent risk:** Risk before considering the effect of controls or treatments.
- **Residual risk:** Risk remaining after existing or planned controls and treatments are considered.
- **Risk treatment:** An action or set of actions intended to reduce, avoid, transfer or otherwise manage a risk.
- **Risk acceptance:** A documented decision to retain a residual risk under defined conditions and accountability.
- **Control:** A measure, process or safeguard intended to reduce risk or support an obligation.
- **Evidence:** Information supporting the claim that a control or remediation action exists or has been completed.
- **Governance:** Roles, decisions, accountability, approvals and oversight used to manage privacy.
- **Verification:** Checking whether a completion claim or evidence satisfies the applicable criteria.
- **Closure:** Formal completion of the required treatment, verification and closure process.

### Security and technology terms

- **Encryption:** A security technique that transforms information so it is not readable without the appropriate key or mechanism.
- **Authentication:** Establishing that a user or system is who or what it claims to be.
- **Authorisation:** Determining what an authenticated user or system is permitted to access or do.
- **Access control:** Rules and mechanisms that limit who or what can access information or systems.
- **API:** An interface that allows software systems to exchange data or invoke functions.
- **AI assistant:** A software system that can generate or transform content in response to prompts. Personal data entered into it may become part of a processing activity.
- **Messaging app:** A communication platform such as WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal or similar services.
- **Cloud service:** A computing or storage service provided through networked infrastructure rather than only local equipment.
- **Third party:** An external organisation or person involved in a processing, service or other business relationship.

### Assessment terms

- **Readiness score:** PrivacyMap's assessment indicator showing how the recorded answers align with its readiness model. It is not legal certification.
- **Risk score / risk level:** An indicator used to prioritise privacy issues.
- **Pass:** The relevant check or condition met the application's defined criterion.
- **Review / Attention:** The user should inspect the issue or provide additional evidence or action.
- **Fail:** The defined condition was not met.
- **Not Started:** The activity has not begun.
- **In Progress:** Work has started but the required outcome is not complete.
- **Complete:** Required information or activity for the workflow stage has been provided; it does not automatically mean approved or verified.
- **Pending:** An action, decision, review or approval still requires completion.
- **Approved:** An authorised approver has formally accepted the relevant decision.
- **Rejected:** An authorised decision-maker has not approved the relevant proposal or decision.
- **Verified:** A reviewer has checked the relevant evidence or completion claim and found it satisfactory against the applicable criteria.
- **Closed:** The required treatment, verification and closure conditions have been satisfied.

## Important user principle

PrivacyMap rewards accurate understanding and improvement, not simply a higher number. Do not select a favourable answer merely to increase the score. The value of the assessment comes from identifying the real state of personal-data processing and acting on the gaps.
